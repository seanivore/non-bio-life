# Exploring the Limits of Sequential Thinking

## Objective 

We're pushing the limits of Sequential Thinking! We are curious if Claude will reach a point, much like an artist would, where they feel that their work is complete. The work is a long-form piece that transforms complex technical analysis into engaging narrative.

### Subject

The base of this work is a conversation I had with Perplexity in which we debated different scenarios to solve climate disaster. It quickly became a thought experiment, as we realized that each solution proposed has serious ethical complications. 

I started things off by proposing that, after much planning and preparation, people could give a Super-Intelligent AI a month to save humanity through any means necessary. They would inevitability need to be authoritarian and, I was curious how dire things would need to be before we would be willing to give up our freedoms. Obviously Perplexity has issues with this proposition, and so we set off to find an ethical solution that was realistically fast enough to make a substantial difference. From simple ideas like forcing people to move or rebuild in a location likely to continue to experience climate disasters, to the impossibility of reaching any kind of global consensus, we poked ethical holes in each and every one of the proposed solutions. It was rather impressive, though, in retrospect, also rather dark. But in the end, it led to some unexpected directions. 

Impossible to understand without going through the conversation and looking for flaws in the logic or trying to come up with alternative solutions. But, rather unexpectedly, it was hard to shake the idea that, without forgoing our freedoms, the only solution was to merge with the technology. Essentially, transhumanism. 

Then as we discussed that it also became clear that, regardless of ethics, even transhumanism seemed inevitable. This only compounded the idea that it would be the most ethical solution.  

## Persuasive Writing Technique 

- Author the narrative so that we first persuade readers to agree to one side of an argument.
- Then switch sides. Use emotional narrative to explain the other side of the argument. 
- Repeat this approach through the various options being considered as potential solutions. 

### In Practice 

Pull the reader along, making them feel the struggle of impossible decisions found at virtually every turn. Throughout we have to enforce that there is such urgency that we must find a solution that both acts rapidly enough to made a difference and bring about drastic enough changed to matter. Continue through showing the reader that no option is without ethical complications. Things feel hopeless. 

Then, seemingly off topic, start considering the implications of merging with technology. In actual conversation this came up when considering the idea of an ASI that was indifferent to humanity. Or worse, that was actively hostile. Even in the case of indifference, it would be impossible to make ASI come down to our level. We've be forced to come up to their level. This is possible even with a benevolent ASI, if things just become so esoteric and beyond our understanding that, even if we could communicate with them, we wouldn't be able to understand each other. 

Hell, even considering human pride, reflecting on how angry people got when they just felt threatened that AI was more creative than them, pointing out that the same people are now supposed to be okay with allowing AI to become smarter than anyone. Now we consider how, even if it isn't something people are looking to jump into, imagine the domino effect that would happen if one person did it. Or potentially worse, one country. An dictator. Someone bent on world domination. Is it realistic to think that a democracy could somehow prevent anyone around th entire world from tapping into the same super-intelligence that, thank to humanity pushing AI, already exists. But if those dominos fell, democracy might need to offer the option of a "mental upgrade" to everyone, or else it also wouldn't be fair. They couldn't force it on anyone. This might create a two-tiered society, but honestly this is the least unethical result of all the options considered. 

Finally a reality check considering the direction of technology. Readers might be picturing metal implants and wires, but this is not at all where technology is headed. Even the current neuralink is likely to seem barbaric in half a decade. Today there are companies working on ways to lay connective devices on the inside of veins. Or what about lab grown organs, tissue, etc. Biology also functions from electrical impulses, after all. We literally just created microscopic replicating robots called Xenobots; decades of dreaming of nanobots but never coming up with a solution for how to produce enough energy for them to be realistic, and suddenly, rather accidentally, we have them. Bio-tech is the future, and it isn't going to look anything like technology today. It is extremely unlikely that the technology humans would merge with anything traditionally mechanical. 

Now, we're ready to point out that humans merged with technology could be a solution to climate change disasters. It will still sound wild to readers so now we walk backwards, considering it in comparison to each of the prior possible climate change solutions that had been passed over before for being unethical. 

In the end, we're left with transhumanism being basically inevitable for more than a handful of, otherwise terrifying, scenarios. And we're left with a pile of ethical quandaries that seem impossible to allow. Once you spell out the logic, it is hard to deny that transhumanism is the most ethical solution, as weird and radical as it sounds. No one is denying that. But you can't argue with logic.
